# Scaling Up or Shutting Out? Firm Size and Female Employment in India

## Overview

This project examines the relationship between **firm size and female employment in India's manufacturing sector**, with a particular focus on whether larger manufacturing firms employ a higher share of blue-collar women workers.

The analysis uses firm-level data from the **Annual Survey of Industries (ASI) 2023–24**, covering approximately **48,900 manufacturing factories**. The project combines descriptive analysis, exploratory data analysis, correlation analysis, and econometric regression techniques to investigate the relationship between firm size and female employment.

## Research Question

**Does firm size affect the share of blue-collar women workers in the Indian manufacturing sector?**

The project investigates whether larger manufacturing firms tend to have a higher proportion of women among their blue-collar workforce compared with smaller firms.

## Motivation

Female labour force participation in India remains relatively low, particularly in manufacturing. One potential factor influencing women's participation is the characteristics of the firms in which they work.

Larger firms may have greater capacity to provide **better workplace amenities, improved safety measures, maternity benefits, and more structured employment opportunities**. These characteristics could potentially make larger firms more conducive to female employment.

This project therefore examines whether there is a systematic relationship between **firm size and the share of women workers** in India's manufacturing sector.

## Dataset

The analysis uses firm-level manufacturing data from the **Annual Survey of Industries (ASI) 2023–24**.

- **Source:** Annual Survey of Industries (ASI) 2023–24
- **Data Type:** Firm-level manufacturing data
- **Observations:** Approximately **48,900 manufacturing factories**
- **Unit of Analysis:** Manufacturing factory

## Variables

### Dependent Variable

The primary dependent variable is the **share of directly hired blue-collar women workers**, calculated as:

$$
Female\ Employment\ Share =
\frac{Female\ Blue\text{-}Collar\ Workers}
{Total\ Blue\text{-}Collar\ Workers}
$$

This measure captures the proportion of a firm's blue-collar workforce that consists of directly hired women workers.

### Independent Variable

The main explanatory variable is **firm size**.

Firm size is measured using:

- **Total employees** in the factory
- **Log of total employees**

Using both measures allows the analysis to examine the relationship between firm size and female employment using alternative measures of firm scale.

## Methodology

The project follows a structured quantitative research approach:

### 1. Data Cleaning and Preprocessing

The ASI firm-level dataset was cleaned and prepared for analysis. This involved processing relevant variables and preparing the data for descriptive and econometric analysis.

### 2. Descriptive Statistics

Descriptive statistics were used to understand the distribution of **firm size and female employment** across manufacturing factories.

### 3. Exploratory Data Analysis

Exploratory analysis was conducted to identify patterns in female employment across firms of different sizes and to understand the underlying characteristics of the dataset.

### 4. Correlation Analysis

Correlation analysis was used to examine the relationship between **firm size and female employment share** and to identify whether larger firms tend to have a higher proportion of women workers.

### 5. Econometric Analysis

Regression-based econometric methods were used to formally examine the association between **firm size and the share of blue-collar women workers**.

The analysis was conducted using **Stata and Python**.

## Key Preliminary Findings

The preliminary analysis provides several important observations:

- Women account for approximately **12% of workers in manufacturing factories** in the sample.
- **Larger firms appear to employ a relatively higher share of women workers** compared with smaller firms.
- A **positive correlation** is observed between total employees and the share of female workers.
- The preliminary results suggest that **firm size may be associated with differences in female employment patterns** across manufacturing firms.

These findings should be interpreted as evidence of an **association rather than causation**. The observed relationship does not, by itself, establish that increasing firm size causes higher female employment.

## Research Contributions

This project contributes to the understanding of **female employment patterns in India's manufacturing sector** by examining firm-level variation in women's participation.

By using a large firm-level dataset covering approximately **48,900 manufacturing factories**, the project provides a detailed empirical examination of whether firm characteristics, particularly firm size, are associated with differences in female employment.

The analysis also highlights the importance of examining **firm-level characteristics** when studying women's participation in the manufacturing labour market.
