Scaling Up or Shutting Out? Firm Size and Female Employment in India
Project Overview

This project examines the relationship between firm size and female employment in India's manufacturing sector, with a particular focus on whether larger manufacturing firms employ a greater share of blue-collar women workers. The analysis uses firm-level data from the Annual Survey of Industries (ASI) 2023–24, covering approximately 48,900 manufacturing factories.

The project was motivated by the persistently low participation of women in India's labour market, particularly within manufacturing. One possible explanation is that larger firms may provide better workplace facilities, improved safety measures, maternity benefits, and more structured employment opportunities, potentially making them more conducive to female employment.

Research Question

The central research question is:

Does firm size affect the share of blue-collar women workers employed in Indian manufacturing firms?

The analysis focuses on whether there is a systematic relationship between the size of a manufacturing firm and the proportion of its blue-collar workforce that consists of women.

Data and Variables

The analysis uses firm-level manufacturing data from the ASI 2023–24, containing approximately 48,900 factories.

The primary dependent variable is the share of directly hired blue-collar women workers, calculated as:

Female Blue-Collar Workers / Total Blue-Collar Workers

Firm size is the main explanatory variable and is measured using the total number of employees as well as the logarithm of total employees. These measures allow the analysis to examine whether the relationship between firm size and female employment remains evident when firm size is transformed.

Methodology

The project involved several stages of quantitative analysis:

Data cleaning and preprocessing to prepare the ASI firm-level dataset for analysis
Descriptive statistics to understand the distribution of firm size and female employment
Exploratory data analysis to identify patterns across manufacturing firms
Correlation analysis to examine the relationship between firm size and female employment
Econometric regression analysis to formally investigate the association between firm size and the share of blue-collar women workers

The analysis was conducted using Stata and Python, combining econometric methods with data processing and statistical analysis.

Key Preliminary Findings

The preliminary analysis indicates that women account for approximately 12% of workers in manufacturing factories in the sample. The results also suggest that larger firms tend to employ a relatively higher share of women workers.

A positive correlation is observed between the total number of employees in a firm and the share of female workers, providing preliminary evidence of a positive association between firm size and female employment.

These findings suggest that firm characteristics may play an important role in shaping women's participation in manufacturing employment. However, the preliminary correlation should not be interpreted as establishing a causal relationship between firm size and female employment.
